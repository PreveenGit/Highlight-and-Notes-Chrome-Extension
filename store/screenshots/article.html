Reading

The quiet art of reading slowly

6 min read

Most of what we read online is skimmed, not read. Our eyes slide down the page looking for the next heading, and by the time we close the tab the ideas have already started to fade. Reading is not a race; it is a conversation with someone who is not in the room.

Slowing down does not mean reading less. It means choosing a few pieces worth your attention and giving them all of it. When something surprises you, stop and mark the sentence that changed your mind, then keep going. That small act turns a passing thought into something you can find again.

For centuries, the margins of a page were where thinking happened. Readers argued with authors in pencil, underlined what mattered and left notes for their future selves. The web took away the margins, but the habit is still worth keeping.

Return to your highlights a week later. Most of them will feel obvious, and that is fine. The two or three that still feel sharp are the ideas you actually learned.
